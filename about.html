I turn ideas into interfaces, interfaces into motion, and motion into meaning.

The story

I started the way a lot of creative developers do: taking things apart to see how they worked, then putting it back together with my own tweaks. One of my first such endeavours was when I repurposed a CAPTCHa solver model from GitHub and used it to automate playing a TCG which required solving CAPTCHas. After a lot of tries, it worked as intended and I was quite satisfied with myself.

Since then, I have delved deeper into design and engineering. I like working as a frontend Developer. Lately I have been trying to make fluid simulations that respond to a cursor like water responds to a hand. I give typography mass, velocity, and the ability to shatter. I synthesize sound using cursor movement as input.

As of right now, I am working as a Student Developer at Toronto Metropolitan University's FYEO. But I am always open to collaborations and future internships/co-op workterms.

How I work

Every project starts with a question. I prototype early and often, because the prototype is the strategy.

I care about the details people feel rather than see—the damping on a spring, the dissipation of a fluid field, the two milliseconds of attack on a gain envelope. Those decisions are where an interface stops feeling authored and starts feeling inevitable.

Clarity is not the absence of complexity. It is complexity, resolved.

Beyond the screen
